In nineteen ninety-six, Pepsi ran a TV commercial offering a thirty-two million dollar military jet for seven million points—and didn't realize someone was actually crazy enough to buy them.
In Seattle, Washington, twenty-one-year-old John Leonard saw that the fine print allowed buying points directly for ten cents each. Therefore, he raised seven hundred thousand dollars from investors and mailed Pepsi a check demanding his jet. But Pepsi laughed and told him it was just a joke.
You see, under American contract law, an offer in an ad is legally binding if a reasonable person would take it seriously. So John sued Pepsi in federal court to force them to hand over the warplane.
The judge ruled no reasonable person would expect a soda company to deliver a fighter jet. But Pepsi was so spooked they changed the ad to demand seven hundred million points.
He didn't get the jet... but he made Pepsi rewrite their fine print forever.
